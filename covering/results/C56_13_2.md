## How it was found

Targeting came from provenance, not from the size of the published gap.
The repository records how each entry was established; cells whose value
came from a generic recursive construction have usually never had a
dedicated search run on them. This cell was not one of those, and it fell
to the same sweep anyway.

## Provenance of the 25

The repository's history for this cell holds two entries, both at 25:

    ['25', 'Simulated Annealing', 'Michael Dambmann', '2008-12-20']
    ['25', 'Simple construction from (57,13,2)', 'LJCR', '2009-01-22']

So 25 was reached by dedicated simulated annealing in December 2008 and
re-derived a month later by restriction from C(57,13,2) = 25. It has stood
since. Giovanni Acerbi's independently maintained repository, which tracks
improvements beyond the La Jolla data, was queried live and also shows 25
with nothing newer than 2009-01-22.

Two checks that 24 is not free. The standard recursion
C(v,k,2) <= C(v-1,k-1,1) + C(v-1,k,2) gives 5 + 24 = 29 here, worse than
the known 25, so no textbook construction hands it over. And monotonicity
in v against the published C(55,13,2) = 24 means 24 is the least this cell
could possibly be without the v = 55 entry also falling.

The search is the annealing kernel in `rust/covering_kernel`, driven over a
free design of 24 blocks: coverage is a count per requirement, so a swap
costs only the requirements containing the point leaving and the point
arriving, and three quarters of moves are aimed at a requirement that is
actually missing.
